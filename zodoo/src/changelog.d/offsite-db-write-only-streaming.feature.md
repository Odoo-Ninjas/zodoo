Large objects are now **streamed** to the write-only target instead of being spooled to a file first: `tar | gzip | age` goes straight onto the wire. A base backup of a 600 GB database would otherwise need ~170 GB of scratch space before the first byte left, on the container's writable layer. Because `age` is deliberately not reproducible, the checksum cannot be known in advance — so the source hashes what it streams, the receiver hashes what it got, and the two are compared.

Before uploading, the source asks whether the object name is already taken. That makes repeats cheap and gives us `odoo offsite reset [all|db|filestore]`: forget the local ledgers and offer everything again, where whatever is already on the receiver is recognised and not re-sent. The reset deletes nothing on the receiver — this machine cannot, by design — and the old ledgers are renamed rather than removed.

Base backups are now compressed with `BARMAN_BACKUP_COMPRESSION=zstd` (measured 58.4 MiB → 20.9 MiB on a small database) and `BARMAN_RETENTION` drops to `RECOVERY WINDOW OF 2 DAYS`, because the history lives offsite and every extra local day is another full copy of the data directory. Together that turns "over 4 TB locally for a 600 GB database" into a few hundred GB. The barman image now ships the `zstd` and `lz4` binaries: barman hands the decompressor to `tar`, so without them a compressed base backup fails outright with `tar (child): zstd: Cannot exec` — no backup at all rather than an uncompressed one.
